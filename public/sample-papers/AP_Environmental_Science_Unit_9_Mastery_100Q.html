Essential Tip: Do not confuse stratospheric ozone depletion with global climate change! Ozone depletion is caused by CFCs destroying O₃ in the stratosphere, increasing ground-level harmful UV-B radiation. Climate change is caused by greenhouse gases (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O) absorbing outgoing infrared radiation in the troposphere.

Correct Answer: (A) Ultraviolet radiation cleaves chlorine atoms from CFC molecules, and free chlorine radicals react with ozone to form chlorine monoxide and diatomic oxygen.
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION

Essential Unit 9 Climate & Ozone Chemistry Rules
Core formulas, biogeochemical pathways, and environmental principles required for AP Environmental Science Unit 9.
  • Ozone Depletion Catalysis: CFCl₃ + UV → CFCl₂ + Cl•; Cl• + O₃ → ClO• + O₂; ClO• + O → Cl• + O₂. A single free chlorine radical can destroy upwards of 100,000 ozone molecules before forming stable reservoir compounds.
  • The Greenhouse Effect Mechanism: Solar UV and visible light penetrate the atmosphere and warm Earth's surface. Earth reradiates energy as longer-wavelength Infrared (IR) radiation. Greenhouse gases (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, tropospheric O₃, CFCs) absorb and re-emit IR radiation toward the surface.
  • Global Warming Potential (GWP): Relative measure of heat trapped over 100 years compared to CO₂: CO₂ = 1, CH₄ ≈ 28–36, N₂O ≈ 265–298, CFCs/HFCs > 1,000–10,000.
  • Ocean Acidification Chemistry: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. Excess free hydrogen ions (H⁺) bind with carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻) to form bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻), severely depleting available carbonate needed by corals, mollusks, and pteropods to synthesize calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) shells.
