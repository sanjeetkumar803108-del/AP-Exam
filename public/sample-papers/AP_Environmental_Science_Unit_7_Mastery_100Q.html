APPENDIX FORMULA REFERENCE & THEOREMS
Essential Unit 7 Atmospheric Chemistry Rules
Core formulas, biogeochemical pathways, and environmental principles required for AP Environmental Science Unit 7.
  • Primary vs. Secondary Air Pollutants: Primary pollutants emitted directly from sources (CO, CO₂, SO₂, NO, particulate matter, VOCs). Secondary pollutants formed via atmospheric chemical reactions (O₃, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, PANs).
  • Photochemical Smog Reaction: NO₂ + UV → NO + O; O + O₂ → O₃ (tropospheric ozone). In the presence of VOCs, NO reacts with hydrocarbon radicals instead of destroying O₃, allowing toxic ozone and photochemical oxidants to accumulate.
  • Thermal Inversion: A meteorological phenomenon where a layer of warm air traps cooler, denser air beneath it. Topographic valley basins trap particulate matter and combustion pollutants, preventing vertical dispersion and causing hazardous air quality spikes.
  • Acid Deposition Chemistry: SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ / H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid); NOx + H₂O → HNO₃ (nitric acid). Acid deposition (pH < 5.6) leaches toxic aluminum (Al³⁺) from soils into waterways and strips essential calcium and magnesium plant nutrients.
  • Major Industrial Emission Scrubbers: Wet scrubbers spray alkaline slurry (limestone CaCO₃) into exhaust flues to neutralize and precipitate SO₂ as calcium sulfate (gypsum). Electrostatic precipitators apply high voltage to charge and capture fine particulate matter.
