Correct answer: (B) 8 chromosomes and 16 sister chromatids
STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION
• Step 1: In somatic cells of this organism, 2n = 16, meaning there are 8 homologous pairs of chromosomes. Following DNA replication in the pre-meiotic S-phase, the cell contains 16 replicated chromosomes comprising 32 sister chromatids.
• Step 2: During Meiosis I (the reductional division), homologous chromosome pairs synapse, align along the metaphase plate as bivalents/tetrads, and separate toward opposite spindle poles during Anaphase I.
• Step 3: At the completion of Telophase I, each emerging daughter nucleus receives exactly one set of replicated homologous chromosomes. Therefore, each daughter cell has n = 8 chromosomes, but each chromosome still consists of two attached sister chromatids, yielding a total of 8 × 2 = 16 sister chromatids.
WHY THE OTHER OPTIONS ARE INCORRECT
✗ (A) is incorrect because sister chromatids do not disjoin until Anaphase II; therefore, chromatids cannot equal the chromosome count of 8 until after Meiosis II.
✗ (C) is incorrect because 16 chromosomes would represent an unreduced diploid state, which contradicts the reductional separation of homologues that occurs in Meiosis I.
✗ (D) is incorrect because 16 chromosomes with 32 chromatids describes the initial state of the primary gametocyte prior to anaphase separation, not after Telophase I.
