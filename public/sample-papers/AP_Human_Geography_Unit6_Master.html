• Redlining was the discriminatory practice by banks, insurance companies, and the federal Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) of drawing red lines on municipal maps around predominantly minority neighborhoods, systematically refusing to issue home mortgages, refinancing, or business loans in those areas.
Part B: How Redlining Created Intergenerational Wealth Gaps (2 pts):
• Explanation (2 pts): In the United States, homeownership and home equity appreciation are the primary mechanisms through which working-class families build and pass down generational wealth. By denying mortgage financing to African American families while subsidizing low-cost FHA mortgages for white families in post-WWII suburbs, minority families were locked out of decades of property value appreciation, creating enduring racial wealth disparities that persist today.
Part C: Blockbusting and Acceleration of White Flight (2 pts):
• Description (1 pt): Blockbusting occurred when real estate speculators exploited racial fears by convincing white homeowners that Black families were moving into their street and that property values would collapse, prompting panicked white homeowners to sell their houses below market value.
• Acceleration of White Flight (1 pt): Speculators resold these homes at inflated prices to incoming minority buyers, creating sudden racial turnover that triggered mass panic selling and accelerated the migration of white middle-class families into segregated suburbs (White Flight).

Part A: Define Urban Heat Island Effect (1 pt):
• The Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect is a phenomenon where urban metropolitan areas experience significantly warmer temperatures—often 5°F to 15°F higher—than surrounding rural hinterlands due to human activities and built infrastructure.
Part B: Two Physical/Architectural Causes of UHI (2 pts):
• Cause 1 - Low-Albedo Thermal Mass Materials (1 pt): Paved asphalt roads, concrete sidewalks, and dark tar roofs have low solar reflectance (albedo) and high thermal mass, absorbing intense solar radiation during the day and re-radiating heat into the lower atmosphere all night.
• Cause 2 - Lack of Vegetative Evapotranspiration (1 pt): The removal of trees, grass, and open soils eliminates natural evaporative cooling (evapotranspiration), while artificial air conditioning units and combustion engines pump anthropogenic waste heat directly into street canyons.
Part C: How Impervious Surfaces Cause Flash Flooding (2 pts):
• Explanation (2 pts): Impervious paved surfaces (asphalt, concrete, rooftops) create an impenetrable barrier that prevents rainwater from naturally infiltrating into soil aquifers. Consequently, storm precipitation is converted almost entirely into rapid surface runoff, rushing into storm drains in high volumes that exceed channel capacities, causing flash floods and triggering combined sewer overflows that discharge raw sewage into rivers.
Part D: Define Municipal Fragmentation and Planning Problem (2 pts):
• Definition (1 pt): Municipal fragmentation is the presence of numerous politically independent, self-governing local municipalities, townships, and school districts within a single contiguous metropolitan area.
• Planning Problem (1 pt): It prevents coordinated regional planning for essential infrastructure like public mass transit, flood control, and affordable housing, as individual suburbs prioritize their own local tax bases and enact exclusionary zoning rather than addressing regional metropolitan needs.
Part E: One Regional Governance Model (2 pts):
• Model Description (1 pt): Consolidated City-County Government (e.g., Indianapolis-Marion County 'Unigov' or Miami-Dade County).
• Explanation (1 pt): Merging municipal city administration with the surrounding county government unifies fragmented bureaucracies into a single metropolitan governing body, creating a unified tax base, eliminating duplicate public services (merging police and fire departments), and enabling coherent, equitable regional land-use and transit planning across the entire urban region.

Unit 6 Urban Models & Spatial Systems Reference
High-Yield Summary of Classic North American Models, World Regional Models, and Urban Hierarchy Theories
1. Classic North American Urban Models
• Burgess Concentric Zone (1925): 5 rings radiating from CBD: Zone of Transition (slums/industry), Working-Class, Better Residences, Commuter Zone; walking/horse-car era; invasion and succession.
• Hoyt Sector (1939): Directional wedges radiating along rail/transit corridors; high-income sector along scenic corridor away from industrial rail wedge.
• Harris-Ullman Multiple Nuclei (1945): Polycentric nodes (airports, business parks, universities); private automobile era.
• Galactic City / Peripheral (1960s–90s): Decentralized metro surrounded by beltway ring road lined with edge cities (jobs > bedrooms, malls).
2. Developing World Regional Urban Models
• Latin American (Griffin-Ford, 1980): CBD & Commercial Spine flanked by elite housing, ending at Mall; Disamenity sectors (favelas on steep ravines); outer Periférico squatter ring.
• Sub-Saharan African (de Blij, 1968): Three distinct CBDs (Colonial grid, Traditional market, Periodic curbside zone); ethnic neighborhoods; outer informal shantytowns.
• Southeast Asian (McGee, 1967): Anchored by Colonial Port Zone; Alien Commercial Zone (Chinese/Indian merchants); Western corporate zone; suburban squatter areas.
3. Regional Urban Hierarchy & Central Place
• Central Place Theory (Christaller, 1933): Hexagonal hinterlands tiling space without gaps; Range (max travel distance); Threshold (min population to profit); Low-order (bread/gas) vs. High-order (hospitals/stadiums).
• Rank-Size Rule: P_n = P_1 / n; balanced regional service hierarchy (US, Germany).
• Primate City: Largest city > 2× second city; disproportionate dominance (Bangkok, Paris, Mexico City); colonial extraction legacies.
• Gravity Model: I = (P_1 × P_2) / d²; interaction proportional to population, inverse to distance squared.
4. Urban Socio-Spatial Challenges & Sustainability
• Redlining: 1930s HOLC maps denying mortgages to minority neighborhoods; intergenerational wealth gaps.
• Blockbusting & White Flight: Speculative panic-selling accelerating post-WWII suburban migration.
• Gentrification: Infill renovation of inner-city housing; property tax surges; low-income displacement.
• New Urbanism: Walkable mixed-use design, front porches, rear alleys, Transit-Oriented Development (TOD), Urban Growth Boundaries (UGBs; Portland).
Key AP Human Geography Urban Terms:
• Megacity: Urban agglomeration > 10 Million residents • Metacity: Agglomeration > 20 Million residents
• Peak Land Value Intersection (PLVI): Point of maximum accessibility and highest land rent per square foot in CBD
• Spatial Mismatch: Geographic separation of inner-city low-income workers from decentralized suburban jobs
