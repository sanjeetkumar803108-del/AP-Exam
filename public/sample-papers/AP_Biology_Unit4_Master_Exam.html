FRQ 7: FRQ 7: Molecular Control of the G1/S Restriction Point by Retinoblastoma Protein (Rb) and E2F Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (a): In early G1-phase, unphosphorylated or hypophosphorylated pRb functions as a critical tumor-suppressor brake on the cell cycle. pRb binds directly to the transactivation domain of E2F transcription factors and recruits histone deacetylases (HDACs), actively repressing the transcription of genes required for DNA replication. Serum mitogens (Condition 1) contain growth factors (such as EGF and PDGF) that bind cell-surface receptor tyrosine kinases, activating the Ras-Raf-MEK-ERK signaling pathway to drive transcription and synthesis of Cyclin D1. Cyclin D1 binds and activates CDK4 and CDK6, which initiate the phosphorylation of pRb, leading to hyperphosphorylation (78%) and releasing the molecular brake.
Part (b): Palbociclib is a highly selective, competitive small-molecule inhibitor of CDK4 and CDK6 kinase catalytic pockets. In Condition 3, Palbociclib prevents Cyclin D-CDK4/6 complexes from phosphorylating pRb (hyperphosphorylation plummets from 78% to 8%). Consequently, pRb remains hypophosphorylated and bound to E2F; free E2F levels crash from 100 to 11 a.u. Deprived of free E2F, the cell cannot transcribe essential S-phase genes (including Cyclin E, DNA polymerase alpha, and thymidine kinase), causing profound G1 cell-cycle arrest and reducing S-phase BrdU incorporation from 38.5% to 3.2%.
Part (c): Condition 4 (Cyclin D1 overexpression) produces massive amounts of Cyclin D1 independently of upstream growth factor stimulation; this constitutively hyperactivates CDK4/6, driving high pRb phosphorylation (82%) and releasing E2F (105 a.u.) even during serum starvation. Condition 5 lacks pRb altogether (RB1-/- null); without the pRb repressor protein, E2F is constitutively free and active (135 a.u.), driving autonomous S-phase entry (48.0% BrdU+). In cancer biology, both alterations represent classic oncogenic mechanisms conferring growth-signal self-sufficiency—a hallmark of cancer that bypasses normal tissue growth controls.
Part (d): Prediction: Palbociclib will have ZERO inhibitory effect on Condition 5 (RB1-/- cells); the percentage of cells entering S-phase will remain high at approximately 48% (BrdU+). Justification: CDK4 and CDK6 operate biochemically upstream of Rb; their primary physiological function in promoting G1/S progression is to phosphorylate and inactivate pRb. In cells that completely lack the Rb protein (RB1-/-), E2F is already constitutively free and active independently of CDK4/6 kinase activity. Inhibiting CDK4/6 with Palbociclib cannot restore E2F inhibition because the downstream target protein (pRb) required to bind and inhibit E2F is absent.

FRQ 8: FRQ 8: The p53 DNA Damage Checkpoint, p21 CIP1 Induction, and Apoptotic Switching Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (a): Under unperturbed basal conditions, p53 has a short half-life (18 min) because the E3 ubiquitin ligase MDM2 binds the N-terminus of p53 and targets it for continuous 26S proteasomal degradation. Ionizing radiation causes double-strand DNA breaks that activate the sensor kinases ATM (Ataxia Telangiectasia Mutated) and Chk2. ATM and Chk2 phosphorylate specific serine residues on the N-terminal transactivation domain of p53 (specifically Ser15 and Ser20). Phosphorylation introduces negative charges that disrupt the hydrophobic binding interface with MDM2; unable to bind MDM2, p53 escapes polyubiquitination, and its half-life surges to 165 minutes, allowing active tetrameric p53 to accumulate in the nucleus.
Part (b): At 2 Gy (moderate, repairable DNA damage), stabilized p53 predominantly transcribes high-affinity target genes, notably *CDKN1A* (encoding the CDK inhibitor p21^CIP1, rising to 14.8-fold), while pro-apoptotic Bax remains baseline (1.4-fold). p21^CIP1 binds and inhibits Cyclin E-CDK2 and Cyclin D-CDK4, preventing Rb phosphorylation and arresting 88% of cells in G1 to allow repair enzymes to resolve the damage. Conversely, at 10 Gy (severe, irreparable double-strand breaks), sustained hyper-phosphorylated p53 recruits low-affinity co-factors and activates pro-apoptotic genes, dramatically inducing Bax (16.5-fold) and Puma. Bax oligomerizes in the outer mitochondrial membrane (MOMP), releasing cytochrome c to activate caspases, driving 78.4% of cells into apoptosis. Thus, p53 acts as a life-or-death molecular switch: repairable damage triggers p21-mediated arrest, whereas catastrophic damage triggers Bax-mediated apoptosis to prevent propagation of mutated genomes.
Part (c): In p53-null cells, the master transcription factor is absent, so neither p21 nor Bax can be induced (both remain ~0.9-fold), resulting in complete failure of G1 arrest (40%). In p21-null cells, ATM and Chk2 successfully stabilize p53 (t1/2 = 162 min); however, because the downstream effector gene *CDKN1A* is deleted, no p21^CIP1 protein can be synthesized. Without p21 to inhibit Cyclin-CDK complexes, the cell cannot enforce the G1 checkpoint brake, and cells continue cycling into S-phase (only 44% in G1 vs 88% in WT). This demonstrates that p21^CIP1 is the obligate, indispensable downstream executioner through which p53 enforces G1 cell cycle arrest.
Part (d): Prediction: The irradiated p53-null cells will accumulate massive levels of unrepaired chromosomal mutations, deletions, translocations, and aneuploidy without undergoing cell-cycle arrest or apoptosis, leading to severe genomic instability. Relation to cancer: Over 50% of all human cancers carry loss-of-function mutations in *TP53*. In the absence of p53 surveillance, damaged cells continue dividing despite broken chromosomes, acquiring driver mutations in oncogenes and tumor suppressors at an accelerated rate, facilitating rapid clonal evolution, drug resistance, and malignant cancer progression.

FRQ 9: FRQ 9: Flow Cytometric Cell Cycle Analysis and Checkpoint Arrest by Chemotherapeutic Agents Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (a): In eukaryotic somatic cells, chromosomes in G0 and G1 phases consist of single unreplicated chromatids, representing the normal diploid somatic DNA complement (2C DNA content). During S-phase, DNA replication doubles the amount of genomic DNA. Once S-phase is complete, cells in G2-phase and early M-phase (prophase, metaphase) possess two fully replicated sister chromatids per chromosome, doubling total cellular DNA mass to 4C. Propidium iodide (PI) intercalates stoichiometrically into double-stranded DNA; therefore, fluorescence intensity directly reflects DNA mass, yielding a 2C peak for G0/G1 and a 4C peak with exactly double the fluorescence for G2/M.
Part (b): Ribonucleotide reductase is the rate-limiting enzyme responsible for de novo synthesis of deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs: dATP, dGTP, dCTP, dTTP) required for DNA polymerases. Hydroxyurea inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, severely depleting cellular dNTP pools. In Treatment 1, cells enter S-phase and begin replicating DNA, but replication forks rapidly stall due to nucleotide starvation, leaving chromosomes partially replicated with DNA content between 2C and 4C (82.6%). Stalled replication forks expose single-stranded DNA coated by RPA, recruiting and activating the ATR-Chk1 intra-S-phase checkpoint kinase cascade. Chk1 phosphorylates and degrades Cdc25A, halting origin firing and freezing 82.6% of the population in mid-S-phase.
Part (c): Nocodazole depolymerizes microtubules and prevents assembly of the bipolar mitotic spindle. Cells in Treatment 2 have already successfully completed S-phase and duplicated their DNA, reaching a full 4C DNA content. When these cells enter mitosis, unattached kinetochores cannot capture microtubules, activating the Spindle Assembly Checkpoint (SAC). The SAC generates the Mitotic Checkpoint Complex (MCC), which sequesters Cdc20 and prevents APC/C from cleaving securin and Cyclin B. Because sister chromatids cannot separate in anaphase, cells are trapped in prometaphase/metaphase, resulting in 85.3% of cells arrested with a 4C DNA content.
Part (d): The Sub-G1 population (< 2C DNA content, 48.2%) represents apoptotic cells undergoing nuclear fragmentation and internucleosomal DNA degradation. Doxorubicin is a topoisomerase II poison that stabilizes covalent topoisomerase II-DNA cleavage complexes, generating catastrophic double-strand DNA breaks throughout the genome. Severe, unrepairable DNA breaks trigger hyperactivation of ATM/Chk2 and p53, which upregulates pro-apoptotic Bax and Puma. Bax triggers mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP), releasing cytochrome c to activate caspase-9 and executioner caspase-3. Active caspase-3 cleaves ICAD (Inhibitor of Caspase-Activated DNase), liberating active CAD endonuclease, which cleaves genomic DNA between nucleosomes into 180-200 bp fragments. These small DNA fragments leak out of permeabilized apoptotic cells during ethanol fixation, resulting in reduced PI fluorescence (< 2C DNA content).

FRQ 10: FRQ 10: Receptor Tyrosine Kinase Signaling, Oncogenic Ras Mutations, and Targeted Kinase Inhibitors Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (a): The canonical EGFR-MAPK signaling cascade proceeds in the following sequential order: (1) Extracellular ligand (EGF/TGF-alpha) binds EGFR (Receptor Tyrosine Kinase) -> (2) EGFR undergoes dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation on cytoplasmic tyrosines -> (3) Phosphotyrosines recruit the Grb2 adaptor and SOS (Guanine Nucleotide Exchange Factor) -> (4) SOS activates Ras (small monomeric G-protein) by exchanging GDP for GTP -> (5) Active Ras-GTP recruits and activates Raf (MAPKKK, a Ser/Thr protein kinase) -> (6) Raf phosphorylates and activates MEK1/2 (MAPKK, a dual-specificity protein kinase) -> (7) MEK1/2 phosphorylates and activates ERK1/2 (MAPK, a Ser/Thr protein kinase) -> (8) Phosphorylated ERK enters the nucleus to drive transcription of proliferative transcription factors (Myc, Fos, Jun).
Part (b): Line B possesses an activating kinase mutation in EGFR (L858R), meaning the oncogenic driver is at the top of the signaling cascade. Gefitinib is a competitive ATP-pocket inhibitor of EGFR; in Line B, Gefitinib directly shuts down mutant EGFR kinase activity (phospho-EGFR drops from 340% to 8%), which stops downstream recruitment of Grb2-SOS, dropping Ras-GTP (12%) and phospho-ERK (10%), depriving the cells of survival signals and killing them (viability crashes to 14%). Conversely, Line C possesses an oncogenic point mutation in KRAS (G12C), which operates biochemically downstream of EGFR. Mutant KRAS G12C is locked in an autonomous, constitutively active GTP-bound state due to loss of GTPase activity. Although Gefitinib successfully inhibits EGFR in Line C (phospho-EGFR drops to 11%), mutant Ras continues to signal autonomously to Raf-MEK-ERK completely independent of EGFR input (Ras-GTP remains 375%, phospho-ERK remains 318%, viability remains 198%).
Part (c): Sotorasib specifically targets the oncogenic KRAS G12C oncoprotein by forming a covalent bond with the mutant cysteine-12 residue in the switch-II pocket, locking mutant Ras in its inactive GDP-bound conformation. Consequently, Sotorasib directly shuts down Ras activation (Ras-GTP crashes from 380% to 15%), which cuts off signaling to downstream Raf, MEK, and ERK (phospho-ERK drops to 18%, viability drops to 22%). In contrast, Trametinib is an allosteric inhibitor of MEK1/2, which acts downstream of Ras. Under Trametinib treatment, mutant Ras remains locked in the active GTP-bound state (Ras-GTP remains high at 385%), but MEK catalytic activity is blocked, preventing the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 (phospho-ERK crashes to 12%, viability crashes to 18%).
Part (d): In the wild-type and L858R mutant EGFR, threonine-790 acts as the 'gatekeeper' residue at the entrance of the hydrophobic ATP-binding pocket, allowing small-molecule inhibitors like Gefitinib to dock. The acquired T790M point mutation replaces threonine with a much bulkier, hydrophobic methionine residue. Methionine sterically clashes with Gefitinib, physically preventing the drug from binding into the ATP pocket. Furthermore, the T790M mutation increases the receptor's affinity for cellular ATP back to near wild-type levels, allowing physiological ATP to outcompete any residual drug binding, restoring constitutive autophosphorylation and conferring complete clinical resistance.
