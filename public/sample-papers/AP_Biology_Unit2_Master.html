FRQ 8: FRQ 8: Evolutionary and Molecular Evidence for the Endosymbiotic Theory of Plastids Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (a): The Endosymbiotic Theory posits that chloroplasts evolved from a photosynthetic, oxygenic prokaryote that was engulfed by an ancestral non-photosynthetic eukaryotic host cell and entered into a mutually beneficial endosymbiotic relationship. The ancestral prokaryotic lineage is photosynthetic cyanobacteria.
Part (b): Evidence 1 - Ribosome structure and antibiotic sensitivity: Chloroplast ribosomes are 70S and their peptidyl transferase activity is blocked by chloramphenicol while remaining impervious to cycloheximide, mirroring cyanobacteria exactly and contrasting with eukaryotic 80S cytosolic ribosomes. Part (c): The double-membrane envelope reflects the engulfment event: the inner membrane represents the ancestral plasma membrane of the engulfed cyanobacterium (retaining prokaryotic transport systems and lipid profiles), while the outer membrane derived from the phagotrophic vacuolar membrane of the ancestral eukaryotic host (or modified bacterial outer membrane).
Part (d): Over evolutionary time, endosymbiotic gene transfer (EGT) occurred. Massive fragments of the ancestral cyanobacterial genome were transferred horizontally into the eukaryotic host nucleus. Once integrated into nuclear chromosomes, these genes acquired eukaryotic promoters and transit-peptide encoding sequences, allowing the proteins to be translated in the cytosol and imported post-translationally back into the chloroplast via TOC and TIC translocon complexes.

FRQ 9: FRQ 9: Receptor-Mediated Endocytosis Dynamics and Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) Clearance Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (a): FH Patient Group C exhibits normal surface binding of LDL at 4 °C (1.22 vs 1.25 μg/mg in normal cells), but fails completely to internalize LDL at 37 °C (only 0.08 vs 3.80 μg/mg). This indicates that the extracellular ligand-binding domain is fully intact, but the mutation resides in the cytosolic carboxyl-terminal domain (such as the NPXY motif) that prevents the receptor from associating with adaptor protein-2 (AP-2) and clustering into clathrin-coated pits.
Part (b): Step 1: ApoB-100 on LDL particles binds to LDL receptors on the plasma membrane. Step 2: AP-2 adaptor complexes recruit clathrin triskelions, forming clathrin-coated pits that invaginate and pinch off via dynamin GTPase activity. Step 3: Clathrin uncoats, and the transport vesicle fuses with sorting endosomes. Step 4: The low pH of the endosome (~5.5 maintained by V-ATPases) induces a conformational change causing LDL to dissociate from the receptor. Step 5: The empty receptor recycles back to the plasma membrane in a recycling endosome, while LDL travels to lysosomes where acid lipases degrade the particle, releasing free cholesterol into the cytosol.
Part (c): Chloroquine is a weak base that diffuses into endosomes and lysosomes, neutralizing their internal acidity and elevating pH to ~6.5. This neutralization has no effect on plasma membrane clathrin assembly or vesicle scission (internalization remains normal at 3.75 μg/mg). However, lysosomal acid lipases require an acidic pH (pH ~4.5) for catalytic activity; neutral pH inactivates them, preventing LDL hydrolysis and abolishing free cholesterol liberation.
Part (d): In cells with deficient intracellular cholesterol (such as FH patients or chloroquine-treated cells), the rate of de novo cholesterol synthesis will increase substantially. Justification: Low intracellular cholesterol prevents the homeostatic negative feedback inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase (the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis) and triggers the translocation of SREBP to the nucleus, upregulating cholesterol biosynthetic gene transcription.
