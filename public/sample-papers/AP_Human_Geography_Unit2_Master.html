(a) Refugee Definition and Spatial Distinction [2 Points]:
• A refugee is a person who has fled their country of nationality owing to a well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group, or political opinion.
• Spatial Distinction: A refugee has crossed an official international border into another sovereign nation, whereas an IDP remains displaced inside their own country's territorial boundaries.
(b) Greater Humanitarian Peril Faced by IDPs [2 Points]:
• Because IDPs do not cross international borders, they remain under the legal jurisdiction of their own sovereign government—which is often the very regime persecuting or bombing them.
• International humanitarian agencies (like the UN) cannot legally intervene or deliver food and medical aid to IDP camps without the sovereign host government's permission, leaving IDPs unprotected in active combat zones.
(c) The Asylum Process and Non-Refoulement [3 Points]:
• An asylum seeker arrives at a foreign border and files a formal legal petition requesting international protection.
• The applicant must present evidence demonstrating legitimate, personalized fear of persecution if returned home, undergoing judicial adjudication.
• Non-Refoulement: A binding tenet of international customary law that strictly forbids nations from deporting or forcibly returning an asylum seeker or refugee to a country where their life, physical safety, or freedom would be threatened.
(d) Challenges Experienced by Host Nations [2 Points]:

(c) The Gravity Model and Inter-Metropolitan Flow [3 Points]:
• The Gravity Model posits that spatial interaction between two cities is directly proportional to the product of their populations and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them (Interaction = [Pop A × Pop B] / Distance²).
• New York City and Miami represent two immense demographic and financial giants; their massive economic scale generates powerful mutual gravitation (trade, tourism, migration, corporate linkages).

• States with net population loss or sluggish growth (New York, Illinois, California) lose congressional seats and Electoral College votes.
• Fast-growing Sun Belt states (Texas, Florida, North Carolina) gain seats and electoral votes, shifting national political influence, presidential campaign focus, and federal grant allocations south and west.

Essential Unit 2 Demographic Formulas & Models
Core mathematical equations, transition frameworks, and legal definitions for Population & Migration.
  • Rate of Natural Increase (NIR): NIR = (CBR − CDR) / 10. Expressed as an annual percentage. Does not account for migration.
  • Demographic Balancing Equation: Total Population Change = (Births − Deaths) + (Immigrants − Emigrants).
  • Doubling Time (Rule of 70): Doubling Time = 70 / NIR (where NIR is expressed as a percentage).
  • Dependency Ratio: [ (Population 0–14 + Population 65+) / (Population 15–64) ] × 100. Measures youth vs elderly dependents.
  • Demographic Transition Model (DTM): Stage 1 (High CBR/CDR; stable), Stage 2 (High CBR, falling CDR; rapid growth), Stage 3 (Falling CBR, low CDR; slowing growth), Stage 4 (Low CBR/CDR; stable/ZPG), Stage 5 (CBR below CDR; natural decline).
  • Epidemiological Transition Model (ETM): Stage 1 (Pestilence/Famine), Stage 2 (Receding Pandemics/Cholera), Stage 3 (Degenerative/Cardiovascular/Cancer), Stage 4 (Delayed Degenerative/Dementia), Stage 5 (Reemergence of Superbugs/Zoonotic Pathogens).
  • Malthusian vs Boserupian Debate: Malthus (Population grows exponentially while food grows arithmetically → famine); Boserup (Population growth stimulates agricultural innovation and intensification).
  • Ravenstein's Laws of Migration: Most migrants travel short distances (distance decay); long-distance migrants head to major economic centers; each flow produces a counter-flow; young adults are most mobile.
  • Zelinsky's Migration Transition: Stage 2 experiences massive rural-to-urban and international emigration; Stage 4/5 becomes prime international destination and experiences intraregional suburbanization.
  • UNHCR Legal Categories: Refugee (crossed international border fleeing persecution); IDP (displaced internally within home borders); Asylum Seeker (petitioning for recognized refugee protection).
