Tightly adhering crustose lichens (which have the lowest exposed surface area and highest tolerance) persist nearest the smelter. In the immediate vicinity (<5 km), a complete 'lichen desert' occurs where toxic SO2 eliminates all lichen growth.
• Part (c) Scientific hypothesis: Hypothesis: If atmospheric sulfur dioxide concentration increases along the transect, then the percent cover of fruticose lichens on mature oak trunks will decrease significantly, because sulfur dioxide oxidizes into toxic bisulfite (HSO3-) that denatures photobiont chlorophyll pigments.
• Part (d) Controlled field experimental design: Researchers establish transplant chamber stations at an unpolluted baseline site. Lichen samples (identical species, size, and tree bark substrate) are placed into three chambers with controlled microclimates: (1) Exposure to elevated SO2 only (at concentrations matching the smelter); (2) Exposure to copper dust only; (3) Combined SO2 and copper exposure; and (4) Filtered air control chamber. Photobiont chlorophyll fluorescence (Fv/Fm) and mortality are measured over 90 days to isolate the primary phytotoxic driver.

7.8 Noise Pollution: Marine Underwater Acoustics, Cetacean Masking & Speed Abatement
10 Points Total
Official AP Model Response:
• Part (a) Ship acoustic generation: Commercial cargo ships generate high-amplitude low-frequency noise (20–200 Hz) primarily through propeller cavitation. As large propeller blades rotate through water, low pressure at the blade tips vaporizes water into micro-cavitation bubbles that violently collapse, producing continuous acoustic shockwaves and vibrations transmitted through the ship hull.
• Part (b) Acoustic masking impacts: Baleen whales communicate using low-frequency contact calls ('upcalls' at 50–350 Hz) that propagate across tens of kilometers. Anthropogenic ship noise overlaps precisely with this acoustic frequency band. Masking elevates ambient ocean background noise by 15–25 dB, shrinking whale communication space by up to 90%, preventing mothers from locating separated calves, disrupting courtship mating songs, and impairing cooperative foraging.
• Part (c) Mitigation strategies: (1) Operational speed restrictions: Mandating vessel speed reductions ('slow-steaming' below 10 knots) in designated seasonal management areas, which lowers propeller cavitation noise output by 5–10 dB; (2) Engineering hull and propeller redesign: Installing propeller duct shrouds, using wake-adapted propeller blades, and decoupling ship diesel engines with acoustic vibration dampeners.
• Part (d) Ecological co-benefit: Implementing mandatory vessel speed caps (<10 knots) dramatically reduces the probability of lethal ship-strike collisions with surface-dwelling right whales. Slower ship speeds grant whales greater reaction time to dive and reduce the hydrodynamic kinetic energy of impact, directly lowering blunt-force trauma mortality.

7.7 Clean Air Act Title IV Cap-and-Trade: Economic Mechanisms, Environmental Justice & Recovery
10 Points Total
Official AP Model Response:
• Part (a) Economic cap-and-trade mechanism: The environmental regulatory authority establishes a legally binding aggregate national limit ('cap') on total permissible emissions that ratchets downward annually. The regulator issues a finite quantity of tradable emissions permits ('allowances'), each permitting the emission of one ton of pollutant. Regulated facilities must surrender sufficient allowances to cover their annual emissions and are permitted to buy, sell, or bank unused allowances on an open market.
• Part (b) Cost-effectiveness over command-and-control: Under traditional command-and-control mandates, every facility is required to adopt identical technology (e.g., all plants must install wet scrubbers), regardless of cost. In a cap-and-trade system, facilities with low marginal abatement costs (e.g., those able to switch to low-sulfur coal or install high-efficiency scrubbers cheaply) reduce emissions beyond requirements and sell surplus allowances at a profit. Facilities with high marginal abatement costs purchase allowances. This market flexibility achieves the national target at the lowest aggregate societal cost.
• Part (c) Environmental justice 'hot spots': Because emissions allowances are fungible and tradable across geographic regions, older, dirtier power plants located in low-income or marginalized communities may choose to purchase cheap allowances rather than install pollution control scrubbers. This creates localized pollution 'hot spots' where local concentrations of SO2, PM2.5, and heavy metals remain elevated, exacerbating public health disparities.
• Part (d) Ecosystem recovery dynamics: Aquatic ecosystems exhibited significant chemical recovery, including declining sulfate concentrations and gradual recovery of lake pH and acid-neutralizing capacity (ANC). However, terrestrial forest soils recovered at a much slower rate (decadal lag time) because decades of historic acid deposition had severely depleted the exchangeable pool of calcium and magnesium base cations, requiring extended periods of natural mineral weathering to rebuild soil base saturation.

Essential Unit 7 Atmospheric Chemistry & Environmental Engineering Reference
Core criteria pollutants, photochemical reaction kinetics, thermal inversion physics, and industrial abatement technologies.
  • Clean Air Act Criteria Air Pollutants (SPLONC): Sulfur dioxide (SO2: coal combustion, acid rain precursor); Particulate matter (PM10/PM2.5: respiratory and cardiovascular disease, albedo reduction); Lead (Pb: metal smelting, pre-1996 leaded gasoline, neurotoxin); Ozone (O3: secondary photochemical smog pollutant, plant stoma necrosis, asthma); Nitrogen dioxide (NO2: mobile source combustion, reddish-brown smog haze, acid rain precursor); Carbon monoxide (CO: incomplete combustion, binds hemoglobin to cause asphyxiation). Note: CO2 is NOT a criteria pollutant under NAAQS!
  • Photochemical Smog Reaction Kinetics: Morning rush hour emits thermal NO: N2 + O2 → 2NO. NO oxidizes to brown NO2. Midday solar insolation photolyzes NO2: NO2 + hν (λ < 400 nm) → NO + O•; O• + O2 → O3. In pristine air, NO scavenges ozone (NO + O3 → NO2 + O2). However, reactive VOCs form peroxy radicals (RO2•) that oxidize NO without consuming ozone, allowing ground-level O3 and peroxyacyl nitrates (PANs) to accumulate to peak concentrations in the mid-afternoon.
  • Thermal Inversions: Normally, atmospheric temperature decreases with altitude (environmental lapse rate), promoting buoyant vertical convection of warm surface air. In a thermal inversion, a warm air layer aloft overlies colder, denser air at the ground surface (dT/dz > 0). The inversion base acts as an impenetrable lid, suppressing vertical mixing and trapping primary and secondary pollutants within topographic valley basins.
  • Major Indoor Air Pollutants: Radon-222 (radioactive noble gas from U-238 decay in granite/shale bedrock; enters through foundation slab cracks; emits high-LET alpha particles causing lung cancer); Carbon Monoxide (incomplete combustion of gas appliances, space heaters; odorless asphyxiant); Asbestos (naturally occurring silicate mineral fiber; insulating fireproofing; causes asbestosis and malignant pleural mesothelioma); Formaldehyde (volatile aldehyde off-gassing from pressed-wood resins and furniture adhesives; respiratory irritant and carcinogen).
  • Industrial Abatement Engineering: Wet Limestone Scrubbers (CaCO3 slurry neutralizes SO2 into synthetic gypsum CaSO4·2H2O); Dry Scrubbers (slaked lime Ca(OH)2 injection); Electrostatic Precipitators (high-voltage corona discharges ionize PM, migrating particles to grounded collection plates with >99% efficiency); Fabric Filter Baghouses (woven filtration bags trapping fine PM2.5 via dust cake filtration); Catalytic Converters (noble metals Pt/Rh/Pd reduce NOx to N2 and oxidize CO/hydrocarbons to CO2 and H2O).
  • Acid Deposition & Watershed Buffering: SO2 and NOx oxidize to strong sulfuric (H2SO4) and nitric (HNO3) acids, lowering precipitation pH below 5.6. High H+ ion flux displaces essential base cations (Ca2+, Mg2+) from soil colloids, causing nutrient leaching. Soil pH below 4.5 mobilizes toxic trivalent aluminum (Al3+), stunting fine tree root elongation and causing fatal respiratory mucus hypersecretion across fish gill membranes. Watersheds with limestone (CaCO3) bedrock buffer acidity into bicarbonate (HCO3-), whereas granite watersheds lack buffering capacity.
  • Noise Pollution & Decibels: Sound pressure level (SPL) is logarithmic: ΔL = 10 × log10(I2 / I1). A 10 dB increase represents a 10-fold increase in sound wave energy and roughly a doubling of perceived loudness (a 30 dB increase represents 1,000× energy and 8× loudness). Chronic noise causes neuroendocrine stress (cortisol/catecholamines inducing hypertension), acoustic masking of wildlife territorial/mating songs, and marine sonar/propeller cavitation disruption of cetacean communication and diving physiology.
