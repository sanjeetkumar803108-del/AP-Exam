STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION

Undisturbed root matter decomposes slowly into stabilized soil organic matter, sequestering atmospheric CO2 in the soil profile.
• Part (b): In an IMTA system, three distinct trophic components function in a circular loop: (1) Fed finfish (salmon) consume formulated feed and excrete particulate organic matter (feces/uneaten feed) and dissolved inorganic nutrients (ammonia/phosphate). (2) Organic extractive filter-feeders (mussels, oysters) cultured downstream filter and assimilate the particulate organic waste, converting it into shellfish biomass. (3) Inorganic extractive primary producers (kelp/seaweed) absorb the dissolved nitrogen and phosphorus through photosynthesis. By capturing waste nutrients before they enter the pelagic water column, IMTA prevents algal blooms, localized hypoxia, and benthic anoxia.
• Part (c): Surface fires burn along the forest floor at low temperatures, consuming dead needle litter, fallen twigs, and small understory brush. They leave thick-barked mature trees alive, cycle mineral nutrients into bioavailable ash, and open canopy space for sunlight. In contrast, crown fires ignite during severe droughts when flames climb into the forest canopy. Crown fires burn at extreme temperatures (>1,000°C), incinerating entire mature trees, killing seeds, baking soil microorganisms, and converting the forest into a denuded, severely eroded landscape.
• Part (d): Under a century of total fire suppression, dead woody debris, fallen branches, and dense thickets of shade-tolerant young fir saplings accumulated continuously on the forest floor. These understory layers formed 'ladder fuels' that bridge the gap from ground level to high forest canopies. Prescribed burning safely consumes these fuel ladders in mild weather. Furthermore, fire is ecologically essential for fire-adapted species: it melts resinous seals on serotinous pine cones (jack pine, lodgepole pine) to release seeds, clears competing understory brush, and creates nutrient-rich ash seedbeds necessary for seedling germination.

Essential Unit 5 Resource Management Rules
Core formulas, biogeochemical pathways, and environmental principles required for AP Environmental Science Unit 5.
  • Tragedy of the Commons: Degradation or depletion of shared, unregulated open-access resources (e.g., ocean fisheries, public grazing pastures, atmosphere) when individuals act in rational self-interest. Mitigated through regulation, quotas, private ownership, or cooperative community governance.
  • Irrigation Efficiencies: Drip Irrigation (~95% efficient, minimal evaporation, delivers water directly to root zone) > Center-Pivot Spray (~75–85% efficient) > Flood/Furrow Irrigation (~50–60% efficient, high evaporative loss, elevates risk of waterlogging and soil salinization).
  • Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY): Harvesting biomass at approximately half the carrying capacity (K / 2), where the population exhibits its steepest logistic growth rate, replenishing harvested individuals most rapidly without population collapse.
  • Acid Mine Drainage (AMD): Pyrite (FeS₂) exposed to oxygen and water generates sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄), lowering stream pH and leaching toxic heavy metals (aluminum, lead, iron) into aquatic food chains.
  • Integrated Pest Management (IPM): An ecological approach combining monitoring, cultural controls (crop rotation, intercropping), biological controls (natural predators, Bacillus thuringiensis), and mechanical barriers, reserving targeted synthetic chemical pesticides strictly as a last resort.
