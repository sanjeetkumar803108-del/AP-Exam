In contrast, XP-C cells exhibit virtually no detectable UDS repair (<3% across all doses), resulting in catastrophic lethality with survival plummeting to 0.8% at 10 J/m² and 0.01% at 20 J/m².
• XPC Function: XPC (complexed with RAD23B) functions as the primary initial damage-recognition sensor in Global Genome NER (GG-NER). It specifically recognizes the destabilized, unwound double helix opposite the bulky lesion and recruits the multi-subunit TFIIH helicase complex (XPB and XPD) to initiate local strand unwinding.
Part (C):
• When high-fidelity replicative DNA polymerases (Pol ε or Pol δ) encounter an unrepaired CPD, the distorted base pair cannot fit into their catalytic pocket, causing permanent replication fork stalling and potential double-strand chromosome breaks.
• To prevent lethal fork collapse, the cell recruits specialized Y-family Translesion Synthesis (TLS) polymerases (such as Pol η or Pol ι) via proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) mono-ubiquitination. TLS polymerases have an open, flexible catalytic active site capable of accommodating bulky lesions.
• However, because TLS polymerases lack 3'-to-5' proofreading exonuclease activity and frequently insert adenine opposite modified cytosines (or misincorporate bases during bypass), replication past deaminated cytosine-containing CPDs results in characteristic UV-signature C→T and tandem CC→TT transition mutations.
Part (D):
• Explanation: The TP53 tumor suppressor gene encodes the 'guardian of the genome' transcription factor p53, which normally halts the cell cycle in G1 to permit DNA repair or triggers intrinsic apoptosis in cells harboring irreparable DNA damage.
• Carcinogenesis Mechanism: In XP patients, unrepaired CPDs accumulate in epidermal cells. Error-prone TLS bypass generates frequent UV-signature transition mutations in both alleles of TP53. Loss of functional p53 abolishes the DNA damage checkpoint and apoptotic defense, allowing sun-damaged skin cells with extensive genomic instability to proliferate uncontrollably, forming aggressive cutaneous neoplasms.

FRQ 9: Topic 6.8: Reverse Transcription Quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) Kinetics in Viral Diagnostics Official Model Solution

★★★★ MANDATORY
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (A):
• The threshold cycle (Ct or Cq) is defined as the fractional PCR cycle number at which the reporter fluorophore signal crosses an arbitrary exponential threshold significantly above baseline background fluorescence.
• Because PCR amplification proceeds geometrically (doubling every cycle during exponential phase, N = N0 × 2n), samples with higher initial template copy numbers (N0) accumulate detectable amplicon mass earlier, reaching the threshold at fewer cycles (lower Ct). Conversely, samples with lower starting template abundance require more cycles of doubling to cross the threshold, yielding a higher Ct value (inverse logarithmic relationship).
Part (B):
• In an ideal PCR reaction with 100% efficiency (E = 1.0), the amplicon quantity doubles exactly every cycle, governed by: Fold change = 2ΔCt.
• For a 10-fold dilution (where initial template is reduced by a factor of 10): 2ΔCt = 10.
• Taking the base-10 logarithm of both sides: log10(2ΔCt) = log10(10) = 1.
• ΔCt × log10(2) = 1 → ΔCt = 1 / 0.30103 ≈ 3.32 cycles.
• Because the observed ΔCt between each 10-fold standard is consistently 3.3 cycles (e.g., 19.5 − 16.2 = 3.3; 22.8 − 19.5 = 3.3), the reaction demonstrates near-perfect 100% exponential amplification efficiency.
Part (C):
• Patient A Quantification: Patient A displays a mean Ct of 22.8 ± 0.16. Comparing this directly to the standard curve, Standard 3 has an identical Ct of 22.8. Therefore, Patient A has a starting viral RNA concentration of exactly 10,000 (104) copies/μL, representing a high active viral load.
• Patient B Evaluation: Patient B's Ct value is 37.2, which is greater than the clinical positive threshold (Ct ≤ 35.0). Therefore, Patient B is diagnosed as clinically negative for an active, transmissible infection.
• Biological Meaning: A Ct of 37.2 indicates either trace residual non-infectious viral RNA fragments from a resolving past infection, cross-contamination, or a copy number below the limit of clinical significance (<1 copy/μL), well below the contagious threshold.
Part (D):
• Role of RNase P: Amplification of human RNase P mRNA verifies that the clinical swab sample contained adequate human cellular material, that RNA extraction successfully recovered intact nucleic acid, and that RT-qPCR reagents were free of enzymatic inhibitors (e.g., hemoglobin or ethanol).
• Prediction for double-negative result: If both viral target and human RNase P fail to amplify (Ct > 40), the test cannot be interpreted as negative. It must be reported as an Invalid Specimen (test failure) caused by improper swab collection (dry swab lacking epithelial cells), degraded RNA, or RT-PCR inhibition. The patient must be recalled for a repeat swab collection.

FRQ 10: Topic 6.8: CRISPR-Cas9 Precision Genome Editing & DNA Repair Outcomes Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
Part (A):
• The single guide RNA (sgRNA) is an engineered chimeric RNA consisting of: (1) a 5' 20-nucleotide spacer / crRNA sequence that provides sequence specificity by base-pairing with the target DNA strand, and (2) a 3' conserved scaffold / tracrRNA hairpin structure that forms essential structural and electrostatic contacts with the Cas9 protein.
• Requirement of PAM: Cas9 cannot bind or unwind double-stranded DNA based on guide RNA alone. Its C-terminal PAM-interacting domain must first recognize and bind the 5'-NGG-3' Protospacer Adjacent Motif. PAM binding triggers local melting of the adjacent DNA duplex, allowing the sgRNA to interrogate the target strand and form an R-loop. Cleavage cannot occur without PAM recognition.
Part (B):
• 1. Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ): An error-prone repair pathway active throughout all cell cycle phases that directly ligates blunt or frayed DNA ends without requiring a homologous template. End processing (Ku70/80 binding, nucleolytic trimming) frequently introduces small insertions and deletions (indels) at the break site.
• 2. Homology-Directed Repair (HDR): A high-fidelity, template-dependent repair pathway that requires 5'-to-3' end resection to generate single-stranded 3' DNA overhangs, which invade a homologous donor template (such as an exogenous ssODN) via RAD51 to copy the sequence perfectly without mutagenic indels.
Part (C):
• Data Comparison: In Condition 1 (no template), repair is almost exclusively NHEJ (86.2%) with negligible HDR (0.1%). In Condition 2 (G1 arrest), even with ssODN donor, HDR remains minimal (2.1%) while NHEJ dominates (82.4%). In Condition 3 (S/G2 phase), HDR efficiency surges to 46.8% while NHEJ drops to 41.5%.
• Cell Cycle Mechanism: HDR requires active 5'-to-3' end resection catalyzed by the Mre11-Rad50-Nbs1 (MRN) complex and CtIP. CtIP phosphorylation and activation strictly depends on high Cyclin-Dependent Kinase (CDK1/CDK2) activity, which is present only during S and G2 phases when sister chromatids exist as natural templates. In G1 phase, CDKs are inactive and the 53BP1-shieldin complex physically blocks end resection, suppressing HDR and channeling repair into NHEJ.
Part (D):
• Therapeutic Rationale: The goal in sickle cell correction is to replace the single pathogenic valine-6 codon (GTG) with the wild-type glutamic acid codon (GAG) using the ssODN template via HDR, restoring normal soluble adult hemoglobin (βA) tetramers that do not sickle upon deoxygenation.
• Consequence of NHEJ: NHEJ introduces random 1- to 2-base pair indels directly within the exon 1 coding frame. This causes a frameshift mutation that displaces the triplet reading frame, generating premature stop codons and triggering Nonsense-Mediated mRNA Decay. Because the cell can no longer synthesize any β-globin protein, the patient's cells are converted from sickle cell anemia into transfusion-dependent β°-thalassemia, which is clinically severe. Thus, HDR is the only therapeutic pathway that produces functional hemoglobin.
