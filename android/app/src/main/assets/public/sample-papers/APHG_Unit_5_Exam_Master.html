Part A: Define Soil Salinization (1 pt):
• Soil salinization is the accumulation of toxic levels of water-soluble mineral salts (such as sodium, calcium, and magnesium chlorides and sulfates) in the upper soil layers and root zones of agricultural land, which inhibits plant water uptake and damages crop yields.
Part B: Process of Irrigation-Induced Salinization (2 pts):
• Explanation (2 pts): In arid and semi-arid climates, irrigation water pumped from rivers or underground aquifers contains small concentrations of dissolved salts. When applied heavily to poorly drained soils under high solar radiation and low humidity, the water evaporates rapidly or is absorbed by crops, leaving the dissolved salts behind. Over years of repeated irrigation, these mineral salts accumulate and form a white toxic surface crust that chemically pulls moisture out of plant roots through osmotic pressure, rendering the land sterile.
Part C: Two Agricultural Practices Accelerating Sahel Desertification (2 pts):
• Practice 1 - Overgrazing (1 pt): Expanding pastoral herds of cattle, sheep, and goats strip the sparse perennial grasses down to their roots, while animal hooves trample and compact fragile soil crusts, destroying protective plant cover.
• Practice 2 - Deforestation and Marginal Farming (1 pt): Clearing drought-resistant acacia trees and shrubs for domestic cooking firewood and plowing marginal semi-arid steppes for annual crops exposes fragile topsoil to wind erosion during seasonal dry winds (Harmattan), allowing desert dunes to advance.
Part D: Global Demand Driving Amazon Deforestation (2 pts):
• Explanation (2 pts): The Brazilian Amazon is cleared not for local subsistence, but to supply booming global commercial commodity markets. Global demand for beef in Europe, North America, and emerging economies incentivizes large ranchers to clear virgin rainforest for extensive cattle pasture. Once pasture is established and compacted, land is often sold to corporate agribusinesses that consolidate fields for large-scale mechanized soybean monocultures exported to China and the EU as livestock feed for poultry and pork.

• Environmental Controversy (1 pt): Widespread, continuous planting of herbicide-tolerant (Roundup Ready) crops has applied intense evolutionary pressure on weeds, leading to the rapid emergence of glyphosate-resistant 'superweeds' that require heavier applications of older, more toxic chemical herbicides.

• Standard 1 - Prohibition of Synthetic Chemicals (1 pt): Organic standards strictly ban the application of synthetic chemical fertilizers, synthetic chemical pesticides, and synthetic herbicides, requiring farmers to maintain fertility through animal manure, compost, and cover crops.

High-Yield Summary of Von Thünen Rings, Three Agricultural Revolutions, and Survey Systems
1. Johann Heinrich von Thünen's Model (1826)
Assumptions: Isolated State, flat isotropic plain, single central market, transport cost proportional to distance & weight.
• Ring 1 (Immediate Core): Market Gardening & Fresh Dairy (Intensive, highly perishable, highest bid-rent).
• Ring 2: Forestry / Woodlots (Heavy weight & bulk of firewood/timber for 19th-c. city heating).
• Ring 3: Extensive Field Crops (Wheat, grains; non-perishable, light weight-to-value ratio).
• Ring 4 (Periphery): Livestock Ranching & Grazing (Extensive, lowest bid-rent, animals walk to market).
• Modern Changes: Refrigerated transit, interstate highways, fossil fuels replacing firewood.
2. The Three Agricultural Revolutions
• First (Neolithic c. 10,000 BCE): Domestication of plants and animals, shift from hunter-gatherer foraging to sedentary agrarian villages and civilizations.
• Second (18th–19th c. CE): Enclosure Movement in Britain, Jethro Tull's seed drill, Norfolk four-course rotation (clover/turnips eliminating fallow), mechanization; spurred Industrial Revolution.
• Third (Green Revolution 1950s–1980s): Norman Borlaug, hybrid semi-dwarf wheat & IR8 rice, synthetic fertilizers, tubewells; massive yield spikes in Asia; ecological & smallholder debt challenges.
3. Rural Cadastral Land Survey Systems
• Township & Range (PLSS): Land Ordinance of 1785; 6×6 mile townships, 36 sections of 640 acres; checkerboard grid across US Midwest/West; dispersed settlement.
• French Long-Lot: Narrow linear parcels extending from rivers (St. Lawrence, Louisiana); equal river trade/water access and alluvial soil.
• Metes & Bounds: English system; irregular parcels defined by natural landmarks (trees, streams, rocks); causes boundary disputes over time.
4. Global Agricultural Systems Matrix
• Intensive Subsistence: Wet-rice dominant (Asia), terraced hillsides, high agricultural density.
• Extensive Subsistence: Pastoral nomadism (arid drylands), Shifting cultivation (tropical swidden/fallow).
• Intensive Commercial: Truck farming (fruits/veggies), Dairy (milkshed), Plantation (tropical LDCs export).
• Extensive Commercial: Grain farming (Great Plains wheat combines), Livestock ranching (rangeland/feedlots).
Key AP Human Geography Formulas & Density Metrics:
• Arithmetic Density: Total Population ÷ Total Land Area
• Physiological Density: Total Population ÷ Arable Land Area (indicates pressure on food-producing capacity)
• Agricultural Density: Number of Farmers ÷ Arable Land Area (low in MDCs due to mechanization; high in LDCs)
