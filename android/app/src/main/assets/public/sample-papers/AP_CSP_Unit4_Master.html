Question 04 · Packet Switching vs. Circuit Switching Priority ★★★★★
CORRECT ANSWER: (D) Packet switching allows multiple devices to dynamically share the same physical network cables simultaneously without reserving dedicated, idle communication channels.
  • Analyze the fundamental mechanics of packet switching:
  • • In circuit switching, a dedicated physical circuit is reserved exclusively for a call. Even when both parties are silent, the wire cannot be used by anyone else.
  • • In packet switching, data is broken into small chunks called packets. Each packet travels independently across shared routers and wires.
  • • If one channel is quiet, other devices' packets use that bandwidth, dramatically increasing the efficiency, capacity, and scalability of shared physical infrastructure.
  • • Packets from hundreds of different users interleave across the same transatlantic fiber cable without interference.
Why Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • ✕ (A): Packet switching manages congestion dynamically through buffering and packet dropping, not by forcibly disconnecting users.
  • ✕ (B): Packets can take different paths and arrive out of order; TCP uses sequence numbers in the header to reassemble them.
  • ✕ (C): Reserving an unbroken physical wire for the entire duration is the definition of circuit switching, not packet switching.
Question 05 · Function & Role of Network Routers Priority ★★★★★
CORRECT ANSWER: (A) To inspect the destination IP address of each incoming packet and forward it along an optimal path toward its final destination.
  • Analyze router functionality in computer networks:
  • • A router is a specialized computing device that connects two or more computer networks.
  • • When a packet arrives, the router reads the metadata in the packet header—specifically the destination IP address.
  • • The router consults its internal routing table to determine the best next network 'hop' and sends the packet on its way.
  • • Routers act like postal sorting centers, forwarding packages toward their destination without needing to understand or alter the contents of the payload.
Why Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • ✕ (B): Copyright law and digital rights management are legal and software issues unrelated to router hardware.
  • ✕ (C): Rendering HTML into visual elements is the job of a client web browser, not a network router.
  • ✕ (D): Power delivery is handled by uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), not routers.
Question 06 · The End-to-End Architectural Principle Priority ★★★★
CORRECT ANSWER: (C) Complex application logic and error-checking are handled by the sender and receiver devices (the end hosts), keeping the core network routers simple, fast, and content-agnostic.
  • Examine the End-to-End design principle:
  • • In the Internet architecture, the 'core' of the network (routers and switches) is designed to do only one thing: move packets from point A to point B as quickly as possible.
  • • Complex logic—such as verifying data integrity, handling lost packets, and interpreting applications (e.g., video streaming, email, web browsing)—is implemented entirely at the 'edges' (the end hosts: client and server).
