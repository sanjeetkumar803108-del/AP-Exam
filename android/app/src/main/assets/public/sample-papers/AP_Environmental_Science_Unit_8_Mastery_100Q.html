• (a)(i) Primary vs. Secondary Treatment [2 points]:
• - Primary treatment is a mechanical/physical process where screens capture large debris and gravity clarifiers settle heavy organic solids (sludge) and skim surface grease.
• - Secondary treatment is a biological process where aerobic bacteria in aerated tanks consume dissolved organic matter.
• (a)(ii) Water-Quality Parameter [1 point]:
• - Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) (or Total Suspended Solids, TSS).
• (b) Tertiary Nutrient Removal [2 points]:
• - Point 1 (Nitrogen): Biological denitrification in an anoxic basin where heterotrophic bacteria convert dissolved nitrate (NO3-) into inert molecular nitrogen gas (N2) that vents into the atmosphere.
• - Point 2 (Phosphorus): Chemical precipitation where metal salts (alum or ferric chloride) react with dissolved orthophosphate to form insoluble aluminum/iron phosphate flocs that settle out.

• - 1 point each for two methods: Ultraviolet (UV) radiation (scrambles microbial DNA), Chlorination (chemical oxidation using chlorine/hypochlorite), or Ozonation (O3 gas lysing cell walls).

• (d)(ii) Landfill & Greenhouse Gas Benefits [1 point]:

Essential Unit 8 Toxicology & Aquatic Rules
Core formulas, biogeochemical pathways, and environmental principles required for AP Environmental Science Unit 8.
  • Cultural Eutrophication Sequence: Excessive nitrate/phosphate runoff → algal bloom → light attenuation blocks benthic plants → algal die-off → bacterial decomposer explosion → elevated Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) → severe dissolved oxygen (DO) depletion (hypoxia < 2 mg/L) → mass fish asphyxiation.
  • Bioaccumulation vs. Biomagnification: Bioaccumulation is the absorption and retention of fat-soluble toxins (DDT, methylmercury, PCBs) within individual tissue over time. Biomagnification is the exponentially increasing concentration of toxins at sequentially higher trophic levels across the food chain.
  • Sanitary Landfill Architecture: Engineered with impermeable clay and HDPE plastic bottom liners, leachate collection pipes, methane gas recovery wells, groundwater monitoring wells, and impermeable clay daily caps to prevent groundwater contamination.
  • Municipal Wastewater Treatment Stages: Primary (physical settling of grit and solids), Secondary (biological aeration where aerobic bacteria consume organic matter; activates sludge), Tertiary (chemical/ecological removal of N and P), Disinfection (chlorination, UV irradiation, or ozonation).
  • Toxicology (LD50): The median lethal dose that kills 50% of the test population in a standardized trial. A lower LD50 indicates higher biological toxicity.
