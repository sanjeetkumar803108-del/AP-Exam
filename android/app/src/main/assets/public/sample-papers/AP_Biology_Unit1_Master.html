FRQ 8: Nucleic Acid Directionality, Polymerization Thermodynamics, and Hydrolytic Stability Official Model Solution

★★★★★ VERY IMPORTANT
STEP-BY-STEP ANALYTICAL SOLUTION
(a) Polynucleotide Directionality and Extension Mechanism [2 Points]:
• Terminal Chemical Groups: The 5' end has a free phosphate group attached to the 5'-carbon of the pentose sugar; the 3' end has a free hydroxyl group (-OH) attached to the 3'-carbon.
• Extension Mechanism: Polymerases require the nucleophilic 3'-hydroxyl group of the terminal nucleotide to attack the incoming nucleoside triphosphate's 5'-alpha-phosphate. Because the reactive nucleophile is located strictly on the 3'-carbon, chain elongation proceeds exclusively 5' to 3'.
(b) Alkaline Hydrolysis and the 2'-OH Nucleophile [3 Points]:
• Functional Group Difference: Ribose in RNA possesses a reactive 2'-hydroxyl group (-OH), whereas deoxyribose in DNA has only a non-reactive hydrogen atom (2'-H).
• Hydrolytic Mechanism: In alkaline solutions (pH 12.5), hydroxide ions (OH⁻) deprotonate RNA's 2'-OH, forming a reactive 2'-alkoxide ion (2'-O⁻). This oxygen performs an intramolecular nucleophilic attack on the adjacent 3'-phosphodiester bond.
• Cleavage Outcome: This forms a cyclic 2',3'-phosphodiester intermediate that hydrolyzes, completely breaking the RNA backbone into mononucleotides (0% intact). DNA lacks a 2'-OH and cannot undergo this internal attack, remaining 99.4% intact.
(c) Energy Coupling via Pyrophosphate Hydrolysis [2 Points]:
• Incoming Monomers: Nucleotides enter the polymerase active site as high-energy deoxynucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs).
• Reaction Coupling: Forming the phosphodiester bond releases inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi). Pyrophosphatase immediately hydrolyzes PPi into two inorganic phosphates (PPi + H₂O → 2 Pi), a highly exergonic reaction (ΔG°' ≈ -19 kJ/mol) that shifts the equilibrium and drives polymerization forward irreversibly.
(d) RNA Secondary and Tertiary Folding [2 Points]:
• Intramolecular Base Pairing: Single-stranded RNA folds back onto itself, forming complementary base pairs (A-U and G-C) between self-complementary segments.
• 3D Conformations: This generates localized secondary hairpins, stem-loops, and bulges that fold into complex 3D tertiary shapes (e.g., L-shaped tRNA or active ribozymes) stabilized by base stacking, non-Watson-Crick base pairing, and magnesium (Mg²⁺) ion coordination.
