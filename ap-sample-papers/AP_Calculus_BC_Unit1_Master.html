Essential Unit 1 Formulas and Theorems
  • Existence of a limit: lim (x→c) f(x) = L if and only if lim (x→c⁻) f(x) = lim (x→c⁺) f(x) = L.
  • Three conditions for continuity at c: f(c) is defined, lim (x→c) f(x) exists, and the two are equal.
  • Special trigonometric limits: lim (x→0) sin(kx)/x = k  |  lim (x→0) (1 − cos x)/x = 0  |  lim (x→0) (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2.
  • Rational limits at infinity: compare degrees — lower numerator gives 0, equal degrees give the leading-coefficient ratio, higher numerator diverges.
  • Radicals at negative infinity: √(x²) = |x|, and for x < 0 this equals −x.
  • Squeeze Theorem: if g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) near c and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f = L.
  • Types of discontinuity: removable (limit exists but differs from the value), jump (unequal finite one-sided limits), infinite (one-sided limits unbounded).
  • Vertical asymptote: the denominator is zero and the numerator is nonzero after all common factors cancel.
  • Intermediate Value Theorem: if f is continuous on [a, b] and k lies between f(a) and f(b), then f(c) = k for at least one c in (a, b).
